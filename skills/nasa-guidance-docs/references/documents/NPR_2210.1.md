**Scope, plainly.** Procedures and responsibilities for reporting, reviewing, assessing, and releasing software created by or for NASA (P.1, p.3). Applicability (P.2, p.3-4) reaches software "[c]reated exclusively by, or jointly with, NASA employees," software whose IP rights are assigned/licensed to the Government, and software "[i]n the lawful possession of NASA," including JPL "to the extent required by its contract." "The unrestricted release of NASA software...is prohibited" (P.1, p.3).

**AI and machine-learning touchpoints.** None found as AI-specific text (searched: artificial intelligence|machine learning|autonom|neural|algorithm|automat). "Algorithms" appears twice but in a generic patent/IP context, not about AI: a patents clause allows NASA to patent "the ideas, algorithms, and processes underlying the software" (p.13), and Appendix A's "Software Documentation" definition includes "design details, algorithms, processes, procedures" (p.28). Any AI/ML model artifact NASA produces would be subject to this NPR's release-category review only by inheritance, as "software" (our reading).

**Connections and traps.**
- §1.6, p.7: "The NASA Chief Information Security Officer (CISO) is responsible for identifying security risks inherent in the release of specific software...and shall develop guidance on when an (IT) security assessment may be needed."
- Ch.2 (§2.3 onward) requires an Intellectual Property/Releasability assessment, a Technology Transfer assessment, an Export Control assessment, and an IT Security assessment before release — an AI model's training data or weights would need all four reviewed, not just export control (our reading).
- Cites NPD 7120.4 (this set's NPD 7120.4E) as its sole stated Authority (P.3, p.5), and NPD 1440.6 (this set's NPD 1440.6I) among its Applicable Documents.
- Excludes classified software and pre-1997 unrestricted releases, and does not apply to bug fixes or Scientific Research Demonstration Software tied to a publication (P.2, p.3-4).
