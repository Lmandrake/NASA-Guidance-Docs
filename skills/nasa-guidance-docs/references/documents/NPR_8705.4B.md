## What it is for, plainly

This NPR sets up four risk-tolerance classes (A through D) that a Mission Directorate assigns to a robotic mission or instrument, and ties each class to a set of Agency-level Safety and Mission Assurance (SMA) expectations. Its own stated purpose: to describe "(1) the criteria for Mission Directorates to define the risk tolerance class(es) for NASA missions and instruments, (2) the Agency-level assurance expectations that drive design and analysis, test philosophy, and common assurance practices, and (3) corresponding Safety and Mission Assurance (SMA) planning and implementation elements" (§P.1, p.3). The class is a statement about how much uncertainty a mission is allowed to carry, decided by a human manager — not a statement about how severe a failure would be.

## What it obliges (or advises)

- The Mission Directorate Associate Administrator (MDAA) **shall** designate and document a mission or instrument's risk tolerance class, with justification, "no later than KDP B Decision Memorandum/Selected Mission Statement" (§3.1, p.9).
- The project manager **shall** write Safety and Mission Assurance objectives matching the designated class and get them signed by the MDAA, the Chief, Safety and Mission Assurance (SMA), and the Chief Engineer no later than the project's first system-level review (§3.2.2, p.10).
- Some SMA directives and requirements apply to every project regardless of its assigned class; those are listed separately and do not vary by class (§1.1, p.5; §3.3.1).
- Objectives may be met either by an accepted standard or by a project-proposed alternative: "This approach provides projects with the flexibility to propose tailored and innovative means of meeting the SMA objectives" (§3.2.1, p.10).
- The MDAA **may** decline to assign any class, or set a tolerance looser than Class D, where the mission's own risk tolerance is judged higher than Class D (§3.1, p.9).
- Appendix D ties each risk-tolerance class to a set of named Program and Project SMA objectives, and Appendix E provides an Assurance Implementation Matrix (AIM) for a project to record how it will satisfy those objectives (Table of Contents, p.2).
- Delegation of responsibility is explicitly permitted but does not transfer accountability: "Unless specifically prohibited, responsibilities and requirements may be designated. The stated role or actor remains accountable for its implementation and outcome" (§1.2, p.5).

## Where it stops

- **Robotic payloads only; the crewed vehicle itself is explicitly out.** "This directive does not apply to human vehicles, launch systems, and non-spaceflight aeronautical systems (e.g., airplanes)" (§P.2.c, p.3). A robotic payload riding on a human vehicle is inside this directive; the vehicle carrying it is not (§P.2.b, p.3). Human-rating assurance for the vehicle itself is NPR 8705.2's business — see `NPR_8705.2C.md`.
- **The class is a risk-tolerance judgement, not a consequence rating.** None of the Table C-1 considerations (priority, complexity, lifetime, cost) is a direct measure of how bad a failure outcome would be; a low-priority, simple, cheap mission whose failure would be catastrophic can still land in Class D on these criteria (§C.2–C.3, p.18; our reading).
- **The class is holistic, not computed.** "The considerations provided in the table below are not definitive, nor is any specific mission criterion alone intended to be the ultimate driver to designating a mission or instrument risk tolerance class" (§C.2, p.18) — no formula or weighted score determines the class.
- Does not apply to projects managed under NPR 7120.8 (research and technology programs) or otherwise outside NPR 7120.5 management, though such projects may choose to adopt Appendix D's objectives voluntarily (§P.2, p.3).

## AI and machine-learning touchpoints

- The directive contains no AI- or ML-specific clause; its text is written entirely about mission- and program-level risk tolerance, independent of what builds any part of the payload (our reading).
- The Class A–D scheme feeds directly into NPR 7150.2D's software IV&V trigger: NPR 7150.2D requires software IV&V on "Category 2 projects as defined in NPR 7120.5, that have Class A or Class B payload risk classification per NPR 8705.4" (NPR 7150.2D §3.6, SWE-141, p.25) — so a mission's Class A/B designation under this NPR can be the fact that puts a learned software component inside mandatory IV&V scope, without this directive itself ever naming software or AI/ML (our reading; see `NPR_7150.2D.md`).
- §3.2.1's permission to propose "tailored and innovative means of meeting the SMA objectives" (p.10) is, by its own text, open to any method a project proposes — including an assurance method built specifically for a learned component — provided the Chief, SMA and Chief Engineer accept it in that project's objectives (our reading).
- Because the class is explicitly "not definitive" from any single criterion (§C.2, p.18), a tier scheme that tried to *derive* a risk class from properties of an AI/ML component (e.g., model size, training data volume) would not match how this directive says the class is actually set — by Mission Directorate judgement across several holistic factors, not a calculation (our reading).

## How it connects to other documents

- Feeds NPR 7150.2D's SWE-141 IV&V trigger for Category 2 projects via its Class A/B designation (NPR 7150.2D §3.6, p.25; see `NPR_7150.2D.md`).
- Divides mission scope with NPR 8705.2C along the crewed/robotic line: this directive grades robotic payload risk tolerance, including a robotic payload riding on a human vehicle; NPR 8705.2C certifies the crewed vehicle itself (§P.2.b–c, p.3; see `NPR_8705.2C.md`).
- Cites NPR 7120.5 for program/project management structure and Key Decision Point definitions (§P.4.a, p.4).
- Cites NPD 8700.1 (NASA Policy for Safety and Mission Success) as its authority (§P.3, p.4).
- Cites NPR 8715.3 for the relief (waiver/deviation) process against its own requirements (§1.3, p.5).

## Traps

- Citing the superseded NPR 8705.4A: this directive, Revision B, cancelled 8705.4A effective November 01, 2024 (§P.6, p.4). A document still citing "8705.4A" or dated before November 2024 may be citing a cancelled revision.
- Reading "human-rated" as carrying a Class A–D label from this directive: it does not — human vehicles are explicitly out of scope here (§P.2.c, p.3), and "human-rated" assurance level comes from NPR 8705.2C instead.
- Treating Class A–D as a severity scale: it grades acceptable risk tolerance and uncertainty, not consequence of failure (§1.1.1, p.5; §3.1.3, p.9) — see "Where it stops" above.
- Assuming every project gets a class: the MDAA may decline to assign one, or set tolerance above Class D (§3.1, p.9); such missions still carry the class-independent SMA requirements (§3.3.1).
- Assuming delegating a requirement to a subordinate office removes accountability from the named actor: §1.2 (p.5) states the opposite — the stated role remains accountable regardless of delegation.

— NPR 8705.4B, §1.1, p.5

> "b. This directive applies to NASA robotic programs and projects, including those flown on human vehicles, managed in accordance with NASA Procedural Requirements (NPR) 7120.5, NASA Space Flight Program and Project Management Requirements. c. This directive does not apply to human vehicles, launch systems, and non-spaceflight aeronautical systems (e.g., airplanes), or as a result of foreign collaborations to on-orbit services or non-NASA missions provided to NASA."
— NPR 8705.4B, §P.2.b–c, p.3

> "Class A: The lowest risk tolerance that is driven by technical objectives. This would normally represent a very high priority mission with very high complexity, as described in Appendix C. ... Class D: High risk tolerance that is driven more by programmatic constraints than technical objectives. This would normally represent a lower priority mission with a medium to low complexity, as described in Appendix C."
— NPR 8705.4B, §3.1, p.9

> "C.2 The considerations provided are to be treated holistically with each taken into account to most appropriately designate a mission or instrument risk tolerance class based on the applicable mission criteria. — NPR 8705.4B, Appendix C, §C.2, p.18
