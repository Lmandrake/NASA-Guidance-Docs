**Scope, plainly.** A 448-page reference for program/project managers implementing NASA's space-flight life cycle: Formulation/Implementation phases, Key Decision Points, the Decision Authority, Programmatic versus Institutional Authority (including the three Technical Authorities), program/project categorization, and life-cycle reviews. Chapter 5, "Special Topics," covers Technical Authority origin and roles, the Dissenting Opinion Process (including the Shuttle Discovery STS-121 foam-damage case study), tailoring, and cost/schedule confidence analysis. It contains no AI/ML-specific content; it is cited here as background on the generic governance, review, and dissent machinery any NASA mission — AI-enabled or not — runs under (our reading).

**AI and machine-learning touchpoints.** None found in a header-level survey of the document (searched: artificial intelligence|machine learning|autonom). Given this document's 2014 vintage and generic program-management scope, that absence is expected rather than a finding (our reading).

**Connections and traps.**
- The companion implementation handbook to NPR 7120.5; its Chapter 5 Dissenting Opinion Process is the fullest worked example (the STS-121 case) in this reference set of how a NASA technical disagreement is actually escalated and documented — a precedent for how an AI-capability disagreement might be handled (our reading).
- Only Chapters 1-2 were read in full for this page; Chapters 3-5 and the appendices were surveyed by header/table of contents, not read line-by-line — do not cite specific numeric thresholds or table contents from this page without opening the original.
- Appendices (not inventoried in detail here) include a requirements-with-rationale cross-reference to NPR 7120.5E, roles/responsibilities matrices, and life-cycle-review maturity tables.
