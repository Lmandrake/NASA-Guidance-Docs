**Scope, plainly.** This is the current interim-directive text of NASA's mishap/close-call reporting and investigation process (amending NPR 8621.1D). It implements 29 CFR pt. 1960 incident-investigation and corrective-action requirements and is explicit that "the resulting products may not be used to direct or justify disciplinary action" (our paraphrase of the Purpose/scope section, p.5). Nine chapters cover readiness, response/classification, Investigating Authority selection, the investigation process, the Mishap Investigation Report, post-investigation Corrective Action Plans and lessons learned, and special provisions for commercial-launch, aircraft, and (reserved) spacecraft mishaps.

**AI and machine-learning touchpoints.** None found for "artificial intelligence," "machine learning," or "autonom*" (searched: artificial intelligence|machine learning|autonom|neural|algorithm|software|automat). "Software" occurs only in generic definitions shared with NPR 8621.1D's body text — e.g., "**Control**. An active mechanism used to detect the initiating event or the hazard or both, and enable an active device (hardware, software, environmental, or human) to prevent or reduce the likelihood of the hazard affecting a target" and "**Flight Software**. Any software that is flown on or part of an aircraft, experimental flight vehicle, satellite, lighter than air vehicles, unoccupied aerial vehicle, or space transportation system" (both verbatim). None of these name AI/ML (our reading). This is the Agency's existing institutional process for investigating failures and near-misses, which could serve as a host process for investigating an AI-related incident, but the text itself does not say so (our reading).
