**Scope, plainly.** The standard exists "to insure NASA maintains the rigor and benefits of software formal inspections, when Software Formal Inspections are to be performed on software as specified by agreement or project direction" (§1.2.1, verbatim). It applies to formal inspections of software developed, maintained, or acquired by or for NASA across the development life cycle, "including the incorporation of open source, auto-generated code and test procedures, Commercial Off-The-Shelf (COTS), Government Off-The-Shelf (GOTS), or Modified Off-The-Shelf (MOTS)." It explicitly leaves the choice of what gets inspected to the project: "Projects need to choose which software products they will perform software formal inspection on, which will receive other kinds of peer reviews, and which will receive no peer review" (§1.2.1). Reaches JPL "only to the extent specified or referenced in their contracts, grants" (§1.2.2). Requirements are tagged `SFI-002` through `SFI-027`; modal force in the extracted text is 55 `shall`, 104 `should`, 55 `may` — most of the document is recommended practice even once invoked.

**AI and machine-learning touchpoints.** No occurrence of "artificial intelligence," "machine learning," "autonom*," or "neural" (searched). But §1.2.1 names **auto-generated code** directly inside its own applicability clause — the most direct textual hook in this document toward AI-generated software artifacts (our reading). The hook is a scope statement, not a requirement that forces inspection of such code; whether it is inspected still depends on project agreement/direction.

**Connections and traps.** Appendix B is a presence-only companion (27 `SFI-###` tags) that "truncates stems at the colon and omits their children entirely" — do not treat it as a complete requirement inventory. Pairs with NASA-HDBK-2203 (SWE-087/088/089, SWE-146, SWE-206, topics 8.11 and 7.25/8.25) for AI-code compliance methods. Whether this standard is invoked on a given project depends on project direction and on NPR 7150.2D, which recommends it as a best practice but does not itself mandate it.
