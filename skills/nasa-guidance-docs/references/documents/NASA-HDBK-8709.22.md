**Scope, plainly.** "This NASA Technical Handbook compiles into a single volume safety, reliability, maintainability, and quality assurance and risk management terms defined and used in NASA safety and mission assurance directives and standards. The purpose of this handbook is to support effective communication within NASA and with its contractors" (§1.1). It is **a secondary compilation, not an authority** — every definition carries a bracketed source tag (e.g. "[NPR 8715.3]", "[STD 8729.1]") naming the originating document, and the handbook explicitly defers to that originating document when current.

**AI and machine-learning touchpoints.** Not systematically searched across its full ~175 pages in this pass; this page instead reports the four definitions requested. **Safety-critical** — two inconsistent source definitions coexist here: [NPR 8715.3] "Term describing any condition, event, operation, process, equipment, or system that could cause or lead to severe injury, major damage, or mission failure if performed or built improperly, or allowed to remain uncorrected"; [NASA-STD-8719.24 Annex] "an operation, process, system, or component that controls or monitors equipment, operations, systems, or components to ensure personnel, launch area, and public safety; may be hazardous or non-hazardous." **Mission-critical** — three source definitions, two near-identical: [NPR 8715.3] and [NASA-STD-8739.8] both read "Item or function that must retain its operational capability to assure no mission failure (i.e., for mission success)"; [NASA-STD-8729.1] adds a second sense, "An item or function, the failure of which may result in the inability to retain operational capability for mission continuation if a corrective action is not successfully performed." **Software assurance** — also two source definitions: [NASA-STD-8739.8] "The level of confidence that software is free from vulnerabilities, either intentionally designed into the software or accidentally inserted at any time during its life-cycle, and that the software functions in an intended manner"; [NASA-STD-8739.9] "The planned and systematic set of activities that ensure that software life cycle processes and products conform to requirements, standards, and procedures. For NASA this includes the disciplines of Software Quality..., Software Safety, Software Reliability, Mission Software Cybersecurity Assurance, Software Verification and Validation, and IV&V." **IV&V** — "Independent Verification and Validation (IV&V): Verification and validation performed by an organization that is technically, managerially, and financially independent of the development organization. (Source: ISO/IEC/IEEE 24765)," alongside a shorter unsourced entry, "Test and evaluation process by an independent third party."

**Connections and traps.** 🔴 Because every term here is a copy, **a definition quoted from this handbook should be re-checked against its named originating document before being treated as current or authoritative** — the handbook updates on its own change cycle (Changes 1–7, 2018–2021), which can lag or diverge from the source standard's own revision. Where two source documents define the same term differently (safety-critical, mission-critical, software assurance above), the handbook preserves both entries rather than reconciling them — pick the entry whose source document governs the context at hand, not whichever appears first.
