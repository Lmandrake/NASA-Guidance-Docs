**Scope, plainly.** Implements NASA's Space Act communications function, 51 U.S.C. §20112(a), to "provide for the widest practicable and appropriate dissemination of information concerning its activities and the results thereof" (p.1). Applies to NASA HQ/Centers and to JPL only to the contract extent noted above (p.4); exempts routine internal correspondence, Inspector General activities, and most scientific/technical-information release (p.5).

**AI and machine-learning touchpoints.** Attachment A's definition of "Release of information" (p.12) reads: "To make information available to a non-NASA person, group, or system, including online applications, tools, and artificial intelligence (AI)." It also deems NASA contractors and commercial software running on NASA systems to be NASA persons/systems for this purpose (p.12) — a control point on what counts as releasing agency information to or through an AI tool (our reading).

**Connections and traps.**
- Cited by NID 2800.147 (Public Web Experience) as one of the policies that information-release approvals must follow (that NID's p.1).
- 🔴 Cover-vs-register date conflict (see Revision and status): cite the register's 2027-05-05 expiration, not the PDF cover's 2026-05-05.
- JPL's applicability is conditional on the Prime Contract (p.4), not blanket.
