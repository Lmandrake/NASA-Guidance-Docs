**Scope, plainly.** ⚠️ **This is the examples volume, not the concepts volume.** It walks worked, numeric examples of NASA's Risk-Informed Decision Making (RIDM) and Continuous Risk Management (CRM) processes applied to both program/project and institutional activities: an eight-step Activity-Planning RIDM example (Ch.3 — identify stakeholders, construct objectives hierarchy, identify performance measures, identify alternatives, set risk-analysis framework, execute risk analysis, define risk posture, recommend an alternative), and both a probabilistic and a heuristic treatment of individual and aggregate risk scenarios (Ch.4, §4.3–§4.5). As an examples volume, it imposes no requirements of its own.

**AI and machine-learning touchpoints.** None found in the text (searched: artificial intelligence, machine learning, autonom*, credibility). ⚠️ Because this is the examples volume only, with Part 1 (the concepts volume) not reviewed for this page, a "none found" here is **undetermined as to the handbook's substantive content**, not a settled negative about the Risk Management Handbook as a whole (our reading). The probabilistic/heuristic pairing (§4.3 vs. §4.4/§4.5) is a concrete NASA template for characterizing a risk that resists clean quantification — directly relevant to AI risk characterization (our reading).

**Connections and traps.** 🔴 **Never cite this volume alone for what the Risk Management Handbook requires or concludes** — it is examples; the concepts and definitions live in Part 1, SP-20240014019. Supersedes SP-2011-3422 (2011) and, by extension, carries forward and extends SP-2010-576's RIDM content. The worked-example figures, risk matrices, and objectives-hierarchy diagrams in this volume are largely carried in graphics and tables that are not reproduced here — open the original PDF for those.
