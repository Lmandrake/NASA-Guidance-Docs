**Scope, plainly.** Defines NASA's organizational structure Agency-wide: governance councils (Ch.3), Headquarters offices' mission statements (Ch.4), and Centers' roles (Ch.5), including §3.7 "Participation of JPL in NASA Governance." It is the authoritative source for *who* holds an authority an AI-assurance requirement would be levied by or appealed to (our reading).

**AI and machine-learning touchpoints.** "Artificial intelligence" appears once in the extracted text: §3.6.4.2.d, p.25, lists among the Mission Support Council's subordinate governance bodies: "d. Artificial Intelligence Strategy Board (charter pending)." No charter text exists yet for this board in this document (our reading — the clause names it but states no further function).

**Connections and traps.**
- Office of the Chief Engineer (§4.11, p.47-48): "serves as the lead Technical Authority for engineering," oversees NESC, "coordinates with OCIO and OSMA in the leadership of enterprise protection" (§4.11.3.4, p.49).
- Office of the Chief Information Officer (§4.9, p.44): CIO "is the principal advisor to the Administrator...on matters pertaining to IT, the NASA Enterprise Architecture (EA), cybersecurity, records management, and privacy" (§4.9.1).
- Office of Safety and Mission Assurance (§4.13, p.51-52): Chief, SMA "[s]erves as the lead Technical Authority for safety and mission assurance" and is "authorized to suspend any operation or project" (§4.13.2, §4.13.2.2, p.51-52).
- Mission Directorate Associate Administrators (§4.1) and Center Directors (§5.1, p.71) each report to the NASA Associate Administrator; Center Directors "exercise Technical Authority through appropriate institutional delegation" (§5.1.2.2.a).
- The Artificial Intelligence Strategy Board's "(charter pending)" status (above) means it currently has no stated scope or authority to cite — do not infer one.
