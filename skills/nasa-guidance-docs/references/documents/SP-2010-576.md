**Scope, plainly.** Guidance for the RIDM half of NASA risk management, aimed at systems engineers, risk managers, and risk analysts making direction-setting decisions during program/project Formulation (our reading). Defines risk as a (scenario, likelihood, consequence) triplet across safety/technical/cost/schedule domains, and walks a three-part process — Identification of Alternatives, Risk Analysis of Alternatives, Risk-Informed Alternative Selection (Ch.3, worked via a running "Planetary Science Mission" example) — including named decision traps to avoid (anchoring, status quo bias, sunk cost, confirmation bias, framing, overconfidence, recallability).

**AI and machine-learning touchpoints.** None found in the text (searched: artificial intelligence, machine learning, autonom*, neural). Relevant to AI assurance as a candidate structure for how a decision to adopt or trust an AI/ML capability might itself be documented and argued — the RIDM alternative-selection process does not name AI, but its machinery (objectives hierarchies, performance measures, Monte Carlo risk analysis, NASA-STD-7009 credibility assessment) is agnostic to what the "alternative" being chosen between actually is (our reading).

**Connections and traps.** RIDM "initializes" Continuous Risk Management (CRM); CRM itself is covered only in the successor document, SP-2011-3422, which adds it on top of restated RIDM content — this 2010 document covers RIDM only, not the full risk-management cycle. Cross-references NPR 8000.4A (Appendix A) and NASA-STD-7009 (credibility assessment within risk analysis) — check both for current status before citing this handbook's RIDM steps as the live process.
