**Scope, plainly.** Establishes the NASA Artificial Intelligence Strategy Board (AISB). Its stated purpose: "This charter establishes the NASA Artificial Intelligence Strategy Board (AISB) and sets forth its functions, control, and relationships with other NASA organizations" (§1, p.1). It is described as "the governing board for artificial intelligence-related actions" (§2, p.1). The charter applies at Headquarters and Centers and to "management of Federally owned assets held at (or by) the Jet Propulsion Laboratory (JPL)" (§2, p.1).

**Functions and decision rule.** The Board "serves as a forum for senior leaders to drive AI adoption and governance for NASA" (§5, p.1). Listed functions include reviewing and approving a NASA AI Strategy (§5.a), reviewing AI policies (§5.c), reviewing the AI use case registration process and the annual AI reporting required by OMB (§5.d), reviewing the application of AI risk assessment criteria and mitigation measures (§5.e), and reviewing safety and rights impacting AI use case assessments and waiver procedures (§5.f) (pp.1-2). Decisions: "The Chair is the sole decision-making authority; members and invitees are advisory" (§8, p.3). The Mission Support Council is the escalation path for reclama requests (§8, p.3).

**Membership (roles and offices only, §6, p.2).** The Deputy Administrator chairs and the Chief AI Officer is alternate Chair. Other members: Officers in Charge or deputies from each Mission Directorate; Mission Support Representatives (Chief Information Officer, Chief Financial Officer, Diversity and Equal Opportunity, General Counsel, Chief Human Capital Officer, Procurement); Headquarters Technical Authorities (Safety and Mission Assurance, Chief Engineer, Chief Health and Medical Officer); and Policy Advisors (Chief Scientist, Technology, Policy, and Strategy).

**AI and machine-learning touchpoints.** The whole charter is about AI governance. It points to executive and OMB authorities in §3 (p.1): "Executive Order 13960, 8 Dec 2020", "Executive Order 14110, 31 October 2023" and "OMB Memo M-24-10, 28 March 2024". Its text contains no technical assurance requirements for AI-enabled systems (our reading).

**Connections and traps.**
- A board charter creates no project-level requirements; it routes AI policy review, use-case registration and compliance reporting to a decision body (our reading).
- It cites Executive Order 14110 as authority; check separately whether each cited authority is itself still in effect before relying on the charter's authority chain.
- When JPL is invited to the Board, the charter says the Chair "will comply with NASA Policy Instruction 1000.99, Role of Jet Propulsion Laboratory in NASA Decision-Making Processes" (§7, p.3).
