## ⚠️ Read this before citing: the cover page itself says use a different document
The cover of the NODIS rendering carries, verbatim: "SPECIAL ATTENTION: NID 7120.148, NASA Space Flight Program and Project Management Requirements, is the interim directive to NPR 7120.5F and contains the most recent requirements. Please use NID 7120.148." (cover, p.1). This NID was not reviewed here — treat any clause cited from this NPR as presumptively in force but not confirmed to be the current text until checked against NID 7120.148 or its successor at the register URL above.

## What it is for, plainly
This is the Agency's top-level rulebook for how a NASA space flight program or project is run end to end: the life-cycle phases it moves through, the Key Decision Points (KDPs) where NASA leadership decides whether it is mature enough to proceed, the people and offices with authority over it, and the formal process for raising a disagreement when someone with technical authority and the program manager do not agree. §P.1 (p.7): "This document establishes the requirements by which NASA formulates and implements space flight programs and projects, consistent with the governance model contained in NASA Policy Directive (NPD) 1000.0."

## What it obliges (or advises)
- Run through named life-cycle phases and pass Key Decision Points, producing at each life-cycle review "the appropriate documentation per the Appendix I tables of this document, NPR 7123.1, and Center practices" to show the project is mature enough for the next phase (§2.2.6, p.27).
- Follow the Technical Authority (TA) process in §3.3 and the Formal Dissent process in §3.4 (both "shall," §3.3.1 and §3.4.1).
- A program/project manager cannot be relieved of ultimate responsibility by the TA process: "The responsibilities of a program or project manager are not diminished by the implementation of TA. The program or project manager is ultimately responsible for the safe conduct and successful outcome of the program or project" (§3.3.1, p.45).
- Document and route a Formal Dissent "by the dissenter in coordination with the disagreeing party at a minimum of two levels of management above the original program or project decision" (§3.4.2, p.49).
- Classify a payload by risk tolerance class no later than the KDP B decision memo — this NPR's own Table I-4 (Project Milestone Products Maturity Matrix) carries the cell "Classify payload(s) by risk per NPR 8705.4," marked as a requirement product ("R" column, §I.1.c, p.133), due at the System Requirements Review in Phase A; the gate deadline itself is set by NPR 8705.4B, not by this NPR.

## Where it stops
- Sets when a product must exist and how mature it must be — never whether the product's content is correct (our reading).
- §3.3.4 limits what an Engineering Technical Authority (ETA) can approve: "The ETA cannot approve a request for relief from a non-technical derived requirement established by a Programmatic Authority" (§3.3.7.3.b, p.47).
- The word "AI," "artificial intelligence," "machine learning," and "autonomous" do not appear in a text search of the public PDF; nor does "7009" or "models and simulations" — so neither a model-credibility nor an AI-specific obligation is invoked by this NPR's own text.

## AI and machine-learning touchpoints
- §P.2.b (p.7) brings "ground systems that are in direct support of space flight operations" and "Information Technology (IT) acquired as a part of space flight programs and projects" inside scope — the clause that reaches a software or model component that is acquired rather than built in-house (our reading).
- §3.4 (Formal Dissent) and §3.3 (Technical Authority) are the two mechanisms this NPR provides for a disagreement about a system's behavior — including, plausibly, a disagreement about whether a learned or autonomous component's behavior is acceptable — to travel upward independent of the program manager (our reading; the clauses themselves name no particular technology).
- Because this NPR sets maturity gates rather than technical content requirements, an AI-enabled capability is scheduled and reviewed the same way any other capability is; nothing here raises or lowers the bar for what evidence a model-based decision needs — that is NASA-STD-7009B's and NPR 7123.1D's business, not this NPR's (our reading).

## How it connects to other documents
- NID 7120.148 is described on this NPR's own cover as its interim directive; the live NODIS site-wide notice (read in 2026) stated that all directive updates were "temporarily paused" while NASA conducts "a comprehensive review of its directives and regulations," with "previously issued directives remain[ing] in effect during this period" — whether that pause has since lifted is not verified here.
- NPR 7120.5F's life-cycle review tables and NPR 7123.1D's life-cycle review entrance/success criteria cross-reference each other (§2.2.6, p.27 area; see the NPR 7123.1D page).
- NPR 8705.4B §3.1.2 and §3.1.4 set the actual Key Decision Point B deadline for payload risk classification; this NPR's Table I-4 places the classification product at the System Requirements Review, earlier in Phase A — cite both NPRs together, in that order, for the classification requirement.
- NASA-STD-7009B is not invoked anywhere in this NPR's text (see that document's own page for what does invoke it).

## Traps
- **Two renderings, two expiration dates, same document.** Always state which rendering (NODIS vs. stamped-PDF) a page citation came from, and check the live NODIS record before treating either date as current.
- **This NPR's own cover tells you not to rely on it** — the NID 7120.148 banner. Citing a clause from this NPR without checking the NID is citing a "presumptively current" text, not a confirmed one.
- Table C-1 (the Appendix C compliance matrix) is not presented as a complete requirement inventory, so absence from Table C-1 does not mean absence of a requirement.
- "Technical Authority" and "Formal Dissent" are process mechanisms, not technology-specific clauses — do not read either as already addressing AI or autonomy risk without separately establishing that the mechanism's scope reaches the specific disagreement in question.

## Technical Authority, quoted
Collected here because this is the document that defines the role, independence, and dissent mechanism; NPR 7123.1D (and NPR 7120.10B) refer back to this NPR for Technical Authority rather than redefining it.

> "As part of Institutional Authority, NASA established the Technical Authority (TA) process as a system of checks and balances to provide independent oversight of programs and projects in support of safety and mission success through the selection of specific individuals with delegated levels of authority. Individuals with these formal delegations are Technical Authorities."
— NPR 7120.5F, §3.1, p.42

> "Programs and projects shall follow the Technical Authority (TA) process established in this Section 3.3. ... The responsibilities of a program or project manager are not diminished by the implementation of TA. The program or project manager is ultimately responsible for the safe conduct and successful outcome of the program or project in conformance with governing requirements."
— NPR 7120.5F, §3.3.1, p.45

> "TA originates with the Administrator and is formally delegated to the NASA AA and then to the NASA Chief Engineer for Engineering TA; the Chief, Safety and Mission Assurance for SMA TA; and then to the Center Directors. The Administrator delegates Health and Medical Technical Authority (HMTA) to the NASA CHMO. Subsequent TA delegations are made to selected individuals who are funded independent of the Programmatic Authority."
— NPR 7120.5F, §3.3.2, p.46

> "The Engineering Technical Authority (ETA) establishes and is responsible for the engineering design processes, specifications, rules, best practices, etc., necessary to fulfill programmatic mission performance requirements."
— NPR 7120.5F, §3.3.7, p.47

> "The Safety and Mission Assurance (SMA) TA establishes and is responsible for the SMA processes, specifications, rules, best practices, etc., necessary to fulfill safety and programmatic mission performance requirements."
— NPR 7120.5F, §3.3.9, p.48

> "The Health and Medical Technical Authority (HMTA) establishes and is responsible for the health and medical Agency-level policy, requirements, and standards necessary to fulfill programmatic mission performance requirements."
— NPR 7120.5F, §3.3.10, p.49

> "Technical Authority. Part of NASA's system of checks and balances that provides independent oversight of programs and projects in support of safety and mission success through the selection of individuals at delegated levels of authority. These individuals are the Technical Authorities. Technical Authority delegations are formal and traceable to the Administrator. Individuals with Technical Authority are funded independently of a program or project."
— NPR 7120.5F, Appendix A (definition of "Technical Authority"), p.67

> "Independent Funding (context of Technical Authority). The funding of Technical Authorities is considered independent if funding originating from the Mission Directorate or other Programmatic Authorities is provided to the Center in a manner that cannot be used to influence the technical independence or security of Technical Authorities."
— NPR 7120.5F, Appendix A (definition of "Independent Funding"), p.59

> "Infrequent circumstances may arise when a TA and the program or project manager disagree on a proposed programmatic or technical action and judge that the issue rises to a level of significance that should be brought to the attention of the next higher level of management (i.e., a Formal Dissent exists)."
— NPR 7120.5F, §3.3.6, p.47

> "Whenever a Formal Dissent is raised, it is documented and communicated by the dissenter in coordination with the disagreeing party at a minimum of two levels of management above the original program or project decision."
— NPR 7120.5F, §3.4.2, p.49

> "Management's decision on the dissent memorandum (or oral presentation) is documented and provided to the dissenter and to the notified managers and becomes part of the program or project record. If the dissenter is not satisfied with the process or outcome, the dissenter may appeal to the next higher level of management. The dissenter has the right to take the issue upward in the organization, even to the NASA Administrator, if necessary."
— NPR 7120.5F, §3.4.5, p.49

> "Formal Dissent. A substantive disagreement with a decision or action that an individual judges is not in the best interest of NASA and is of sufficient importance that it warrants a timely review and decision by higher-level management."
— NPR 7120.5F, Appendix A (definition of "Formal Dissent"), p.58
