## What it is for, plainly
NASA policy on how the agency labels and verifies the images, audio, and video it releases publicly — distinguishing "authentic" media (captured directly from the real world) from AI-generated and AI-assisted media, and setting rules so a reader of NASA's public-facing material can tell which is which.

## What it obliges
- §1.a-b: prioritize authentic media over AI-generated, AI-assisted, or human-created media; use the latter only when authentic media are unavailable or the subject cannot physically be captured, recorded, or transmitted (p.1).
- §1.c: AI-generated media "must be permanently watermarked and contain embedded metadata that clearly indicates it is not authentic media" (p.1).
- §1.d: AI-assisted media must carry embedded metadata only — no watermark requirement (p.1).
- §1.f: all STI products and external materials must "clearly indicate the nature and source of all media when used" (e.g., "AI-generated illustration," "AI-assisted video") (p.1).
- §1.i: prohibits using AI generation tools to impersonate NASA personnel, depict events that could be mistaken for authentic NASA activity, or reproduce NASA insignia/logos without Associate Administrator for Communications approval (p.2).
- §3.a: the Chief Artificial Intelligence Officer, or designee, "shall... be responsible for implementing and enforcing this policy" and "maintain a list of approved and/or authorized AI tools" (p.3).
- §3.e: NASA subject matter experts "shall review AI-generated media content for scientific and technical accuracy"; self-review by the generating subject matter expert is explicitly permitted (p.3).
- §1.h: when AI-generated media is used, it must be clearly labeled, permanently watermarked with persistent metadata, scientifically accurate and SME-reviewed, free from misrepresentation of NASA activities, and compliant with copyright/IP law (p.1-2).
- §5: compliance "shall be verified through regular reviews" of STI products and external communications by NASA STI Services and the Office of Communications (p.4).

## Where it stops
- Scope is media and communications only. §2.c explicitly excludes "text-only documents such as administrative or technical documentation, text-only reports or materials such as e-mails, articles, blog posts, and other similar communication products," and anything "not cleared for external release" (pp.2-3).
- The directive gives working definitions of "AI-generated" and "AI-assisted" media (Attachment A, pp.4-5) but no measurable test for which bucket a given piece of content falls into — the human/AI boundary is a judgment call the text leaves open (our reading).
- Lapsed on its own cover text as of the date this page was written; whether it is in force now depends entirely on the OPD register, which a reader of the PDF alone has no way to discover.

## AI and machine-learning touchpoints
- The directive is itself an AI-specific control, but one aimed at generative-AI *output* (an image, a video, an audio clip) rather than at how an AI system behaves internally — it regulates artifacts, not models (our reading).
- It is the one document reviewed in this set that names a "Chief Artificial Intelligence Officer" with enforcement authority (§3.a, p.3) and that cites a companion document, "NASA Guidance from the Chief AI Officer on using Generative AI" (§4.j, p.4), evidence that NASA has stood up an AI-specific accountable office, at least for this media-use scope (our reading).
- Contrast, not requoted here (see `NASA-HDBK-2203.md` in this reference set): Software Engineering Handbook topic 7.25 §6 reaches the opposite conclusion for source code — no marking of AI-generated code is necessary. The two documents cover different artifact classes (external media versus software), so this is not NASA contradicting itself on one question, but it does mean "mark the AI's output" is not a uniform NASA position across artifact types (our reading).

## How it connects to other documents
- Implements/references NPD 1383.1 (Release and Management of Audiovisual Products), NPD 2200 and NPR 2200.2 (STI), NID 1380.151 (NASA Communications), NPD 2800.1 (Managing Information Technology, for approved tools), and NASA-STD 2822 (Still and Motion Imagery Metadata Standard) — §4, pp.3-4.
- Cites "NASA Guidance from the Chief AI Officer on using Generative AI" as an applicable document; not reviewed here (§4.j, p.4).

## Traps
- The cover and interior text both read "NPD 1383.155," but the NODIS OPD register calls it "NID 1383.155." Cite it as "NID/NPD 1383.155" — the identical pattern recurs on NID 2800.149 in this same reference set.
- Do not trust the cover's printed expiration date alone for a NID. The OPD register's Subject-cell extension note is the only place the current expiration lives, because NASA does not reissue the PDF when a NID is extended.
- "AI-generated" and "AI-assisted" are not simply "watermarked" versus "not watermarked" — assisted media still needs embedded metadata, just not a watermark (§1.c vs. §1.d, p.1).

## Verified quotes
> "all AI-generated media must be permanently watermarked and contain embedded metadata that clearly indicates it is not authentic media."
— NID/NPD 1383.155, §1.c, p.1

> "The Chief Artificial Intelligence Officer, or designee, shall: 1. be responsible for implementing and enforcing this policy 2. Maintain a list of approved and/or authorized AI tools"
— NID/NPD 1383.155, §3.a, p.3

> "It is not applicable to text-only documents such as administrative or technical documentation, text-only reports or materials such as e-mails, articles, blog posts, and other similar communication products. It is not applicable to internal only materials not cleared for external release."
— NID/NPD 1383.155, §2.c, p.3

> "AI-generated Media, also known as synthetic media, refers to content (such as images, audio, video, etc.) that is created or modified wholly or partially using generative artificial intelligence tools (e.g., large language models, image generators, music composition AIs), where the AI system contributes substantive creative content... rather than simply assisting in mechanical or editorial tasks."
— NID/NPD 1383.155, Attachment A, p.4
