**Scope, plainly.** This NPR sets risk-based, tailorable quality-assurance requirements for developing, manufacturing, processing, and supply-chain-managing mission *hardware*. Applicability: "This directive is applicable to NASA Headquarters and NASA Centers... This language applies to the Jet Propulsion Laboratory (a Federally-Funded Research and Development Center), other contractors, recipients of grants, cooperative agreements, or other agreements only to the extent specified or referenced in the applicable contracts, grants, or agreements" (verbatim, P.2). Critically, it explicitly excludes software, facilities, and IT from its own scope — see the AI touchpoint below.

**AI and machine-learning touchpoints.** None found for "artificial intelligence," "machine learning," or "autonom*" (searched: artificial intelligence|machine learning|autonom|neural|algorithm|software|automat). The notable finding is a self-exclusion: "**This directive does not apply to quality assurance functions related to software.** See NASA-STD-8739.8, Software Assurance and Software Safety Standard, and NPR 7150.2, NASA Software Engineering Requirements, for software assurance functions" (verbatim, P.2). It also excludes "quality assurance functions related to the acquisition of NASA institutional facilities" and "information technology (IT) services including procurements of commercial IT equipment that is not mission hardware" (both verbatim, P.2). So this NPR affirmatively routes any software — and by extension any AI-enabled software component — to NASA-STD-8739.8 and NPR 7150.2 rather than covering it itself (our reading).

**Connections and traps.**
- 🔴 Do not cite this NPR for anything software- or AI-related: its own applicability clause excludes software QA, pointing instead to NASA-STD-8739.8 and NPR 7150.2.
- Appendix F gives recommended life-cycle review criteria and deliverables by milestone (MCR, SRR, PDR/CDR, PRR, KDP C, SIR/KDP D, ORR/LRR/KDP E) — a structural pattern (risk-based, criticality-driven, tailorable requirements across a program/project life cycle) that we flag (our reading) as a possible model for how AI-specific assurance requirements might eventually be organized, even though this NPR's own scope is explicitly hardware (our reading, not a claim about current coverage).
- Chapter 5 (Supply Chain Risk Management) requires GIDEP risk screening of suppliers — the same GIDEP mechanism documented in `NPR_8735.1D.md`.
