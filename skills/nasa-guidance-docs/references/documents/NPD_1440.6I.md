**Scope, plainly.** NASA-wide policy for making, preserving, and disposing of Agency records in any format, under the Federal Records Act (§1, p.1). Applicability (§2, p.1-2) reaches "the Jet Propulsion Laboratory (JPL)...and parties to agreements who create and/or maintain records for, or on behalf of, NASA as specified or referenced in the appropriate contracts, grants, or agreements."

**AI and machine-learning touchpoints.** None found by name in the text (searched: artificial intelligence|machine learning|autonom|neural|algorithm|software|automat). §5, p.3 comes closest without naming AI: the CIO "shall...Ensure appropriate consideration and incorporation of records management requirements when initiating Agency use of new technologies such as cloud computing and social media applications" — a generic new-technology clause that would reach an AI system's records only by inheritance, not by any AI-specific text (our reading).

**Connections and traps.**
- §5, p.3: "The NASA Chief Information Officer (CIO) shall: Exercise management oversight of the records management function for the Agency...Establish Agency processes to ensure that records management, preservation, and disposition are addressed in the requirements development or acquisition phase for...new or significantly revised information systems in compliance with 36 CFR 1236.12."
- Implements through, and is implemented by, NPR 1441.1E (NASA Records Management Program Requirements) — the companion NPR in this same batch.
- Cites NRRS 1441.1 (NASA Records Retention Schedules) for disposition schedules, not reproduced here.
- Any argument that an AI system's logs, model artifacts, or training data are "NASA records" subject to this NPD is an inheritance argument (our reading) — the directive does not say so itself.
