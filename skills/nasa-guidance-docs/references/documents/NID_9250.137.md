**Scope, plainly.** This NID "prescribes accounting policies and procedures" for NASA's Property, Plant, and Equipment (PP&E) and Operating Materials and Supplies (OM&S) — capitalization criteria, valuation, depreciation, and disposal (p.36, Ch.5 overview language, paraphrase of applicability). Chapters 1-4 cover general PP&E and real property; Chapter 5 covers Internal Use Software (IUS); Chapters 6-8 cover capital leases, heritage assets, and operating materials and supplies (p.7-p.40+).

**AI and machine-learning touchpoints.** Searched: artificial intelligence|machine learning|autonom|neural|algorithm|software|automat. The document is primarily about accounting for software and other assets, not AI/ML technically, but Chapter 5 §5.3 (p.36) draws a capitalization-exclusion line that touches obliquely on algorithmic work: "NASA shall not capitalize: Software developed as part of a research effort (i.e. algorithm)." That software must instead be expensed as an R&D cost (§5.4.1, p.37). This is an accounting-treatment rule about how development cost gets booked, not a technical-assurance requirement, and no further NASA text here distinguishes a "research algorithm" from a fielded AI/ML system (our reading).

**Connections and traps.**
- Chapter 5 cross-references SFFAS No. 10, *Accounting for Internal Use Software* (p.36), as the federal accounting standard behind NASA's own capitalization criteria — an external accounting-standards body, not a NASA technical authority.
- 🔴 **The cover's own "Expiration Date: May 18, 2021" equals its "Effective Date,"** then separately claims an extension to 2025 — read this as the PDF's un-reissued cover, not evidence the NID was ever briefly expired on day one.
- Only Chapter 1 (overview) and Chapter 5 (Internal Use Software) were read closely; Chapters 2-4 and 6-8 were skimmed by heading only and their four detected tables were not inspected.
