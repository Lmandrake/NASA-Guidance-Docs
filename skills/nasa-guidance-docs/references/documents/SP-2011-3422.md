**Scope, plainly.** The successor/companion to NASA/SP-2010-576 (the RIDM Handbook): restates RIDM (same three-part process, same running "Planetary Science Mission" example, Ch.1–Ch.3) and adds the full Continuous Risk Management back end — the five cyclical CRM functions, Identify/Analyze/Plan/Track/Control, supported by Communicate and Document (Ch.4). Introduces the risk-statement structure CONDITION/DEPARTURE/ASSET/CONSEQUENCE, risk tolerance targets and risk/margin burn-down schedules, and risk response options (Accept/Mitigate/Watch/Research/Elevate/Close).

**AI and machine-learning touchpoints.** None found in the text (searched: artificial intelligence, machine learning, autonom*, neural). As with SP-2010-576, relevant to AI assurance as NASA's model for both deciding (RIDM) and then tracking through implementation (CRM) a risk such as one arising from an adopted AI/ML capability (our reading) — but this is now a superseded version of that model.

**Connections and traps.** 🔴 **Do not cite this as the current Risk Management Handbook** — cite NASA/SP-20240014019 (Part 1, Concepts and Principles, 303pp) and NASA/SP-20240014326 (Part 2, Application Examples, 89pp), both dated November 2024, instead. CRM's applicability to institutional, enterprise, and Agency-wide strategic risks is flagged in this document's own text as "needing further development" (§4, near end) — a gap that may or may not have been closed in the 2024 revision; not checked here.
