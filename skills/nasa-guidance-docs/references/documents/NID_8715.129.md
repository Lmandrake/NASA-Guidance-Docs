**Scope, plainly.** This short (6-page) NID implements NASA's Article IX (Outer Space Treaty) obligations for both forward contamination of Mars and backward contamination of the Earth-Moon system by returning human missions, cancelling the prior NPI 8020.7 (our paraphrase, p.2). It states NASA's agreement with the COSPAR position that safeguarding Earth from backward contamination is the highest priority, while acknowledging that human-associated contamination on Mars "cannot be fully contained because humans cannot operate in entirely closed systems" (our paraphrase, p.4). It commits NASA to a risk-informed research program — using the International Space Station as a biological-monitoring testbed and possibly a precursor in-situ experiment at the human landing site — to close identified knowledge and capability gaps over time, and to making resulting data public (p.5).

- Distinctive content: unlike its sibling NID 8715.128 (a straightforward lunar categorization), this directive concedes that full compliance (closed-system containment) is physically impossible for crewed Mars missions and substitutes a research program to close knowledge gaps — a "we cannot fully verify this, so we are managing the risk" structure that may be a useful precedent to compare against (our reading).
