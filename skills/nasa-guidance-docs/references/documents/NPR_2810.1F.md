## What it is for, plainly
NASA's Agency-wide information-security rulebook, organized around the five NIST Cybersecurity Framework functions — Identify, Protect, Detect, Respond, Recover. It sets the mandatory controls (227 "shall" clauses in the extracted text) for protecting NASA's information and information systems, and names the roles accountable for each control.

## What it obliges
- §1.2.3.4: "The SAISO shall:" carry out responsibilities delegated from the NASA CIO under the Federal Information Security Modernization Act (FISMA); manage the NASA Cybersecurity and Privacy Program; select Center CISOs and Cybersecurity Risk Managers; serve as the NASA CIO's primary liaison with Center CISOs, Authorizing Officials (AOs), Information System Owners (ISOs), and Information System Security Officers (p.12).
- §1.2.3.5: "A Center CISO shall enforce the Cybersecurity and Privacy Program at the Center level" (p.12).
- §1.2.3.10: "An ISO shall:" acquire, develop, integrate, operate, modify, maintain, and dispose of information systems; ensure system-level implementation of Agency and Center requirements; categorize systems by criticality and sensitivity; allocate resources based on risk assessment (pp.13-14).
- §1.2.3.8: "An AO shall:" formally assume responsibility for an information system's operation at an acceptable level of risk, and allocate sufficient resources to protect it (p.13).
- §2.6 Supply Chain Risk Management (p.23), §3.3 Data Security (p.27), and §4.2 Security Continuous Monitoring (p.33) are the chapter-level control sets for, respectively, vetting where information-system components come from, protecting data at rest/in transit, and ongoing monitoring of a system's security posture.
- Appendix C's Requirements Matrices (starting p.46, "C.1 AO" through at least "C.16 IO") index every numbered clause against the role it binds — 246 asserted child edges across 317 distinct references in our count (not NASA's own stated figure).
- §1.2.3.8: "An AO shall:... Assign Authorizing Official Designated Representatives (AODRs), as necessary," and may not further delegate the risk-acceptance role of AO itself once assumed (p.13).
- §2.1.2.1-2.1.2.2: "The NASA SAISO shall ensure the maintenance of a NASA-wide information system inventory in the NASA system of record (i.e., RISCS)," and an ISO must maintain its own component's inventory monthly and provide it "to the Office of the Chief Information Officer (OCIO) in such manner and format that the SAISO determines" (p.16).

## Where it stops
- §P.2.d: "does not apply to information systems that do not process NASA information, and are merely incidental to a contract (e.g., a contractor's payroll and personnel management system)" (p.5).
- §P.2.e excludes Classified National Security Information entirely; that is the Office of Protective Services' responsibility under NPR 1600.1/1600.2 (p.5).
- Reaches contractors, including JPL, "only to the extent specified or referenced in the contracts, grants, or agreements" (§P.2.b, p.4) — the NPR text alone does not bind a Federally Funded Research and Development Center.
- Never mentions "artificial intelligence," "machine learning," or "autonom*" anywhere in the document (our text search of the full document) — it reaches an AI system only as an information system, never as an AI system specifically (our reading).

## AI and machine-learning touchpoints
- Appendix A, p.40: "Least Privilege. The concept of limiting the flexibility of use an information system user or component has, to the degree necessary to perform a specified role." The definition's own wording already reaches a non-human "component," not only a human "user" (verified verbatim, our reading of its significance).
- Appendix A, p.40 also defines "NASA User" as "Any explicitly authorized patron of a NASA information system" — no personhood qualifier in the definition itself (our reading).
- §2.6 Supply Chain Risk Management (p.23) is the clause set an AI system's training data, model artifacts, or third-party model provenance would most plausibly have to satisfy, since the chapter governs how components of any kind enter a NASA information system (our reading — the chapter's own text does not mention AI or models).
- §3.3 Data Security (p.27) and §4.2 Security Continuous Monitoring (p.33) are the general controls an AI system's training and operational data, and its runtime behavior, would inherit as an information system — again by inheritance, not by any AI-specific clause (our reading).

## How it connects to other documents
- Implements NPD 2810.1F, NASA Information Security Policy (the policy-level directive this NPR carries out) — §1.1.1.1, p.9.
- Defers to NPR 1600.1 and NPR 1600.2 for Classified National Security Information, which this NPR explicitly excludes (§P.2.e, p.5).
- Cites NIST SP 800-53, NIST SP 800-37, NIST SP 800-160, NIST SP 800-161, and DHS Binding Operational Directive 18-02 (Securing High Value Assets) among its reference set (Appendix D).

## Traps
- The cover's expiration date appears truncated at "January 03,"; do not read that as evidence of a missing year — the NODIS register gives 2027-01-03.
- "SAISO," "CISO," and "ISO" are three distinct roles with distinct, non-overlapping "shall" clauses (§1.2.3.4, §1.2.3.5, §1.2.3.10) — do not treat them as interchangeable titles for "the security person."
- Appendix C's matrix columns ("Related Roles," "Additional Resources") are easy to weld onto the wrong requirement's text if extracting by line rather than by column position; this reference set does not reproduce the matrix content, only its structure.
