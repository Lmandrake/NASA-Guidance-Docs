## What it is for, plainly
This is NASA's Agency-wide rulebook for how systems engineering is done: the technical processes a program or project runs (deciding what the system must do, breaking that down into a design, building it, checking it, and confirming it actually works for the people who need it), and the life-cycle reviews and decision gates that pace that work. It names 69 numbered requirements (tagged `SE-01` through `SE-69`), each binding "The project manager shall..." or similarly-worded. Where NPR 7150.2D governs software specifically and NPR 7120.5F governs programmatic/management structure, this NPR governs the engineering process itself — the processes any machine-learning or autonomous component would be engineered through.

## What it obliges (or advises)
- Run 17 named "common technical processes" (stakeholder expectations, technical requirements definition, logical decomposition, design solution definition, product implementation, integration, verification, validation, transition, plus crosscutting technical management processes), each with its own `SE-nn` requirement in Chapter 3.
- Verify a product: "Program/Project Managers shall identify and implement an ETA-approved Product Verification process ... for verification of end products generated by the product implementation process or product integration process against their design solution definitions" (§3.2.8.1, `SE-13`, p.26).
- Validate a product: "Program/Project Managers shall identify and implement an ETA-approved Product Validation process ... for validation of end products ... against their stakeholder expectations" (§3.2.9.1, `SE-14`, p.26).
- Document tailoring (relief from a requirement, via waiver or deviation) and customizing (how a requirement is implemented) in the program/project's Systems Engineering Management Plan (SEMP) or equivalent, "along with supporting rationale that includes the risk evaluation and documented approvals through the Center's ETA [Engineering Technical Authority] process" (§2.2.1.3, p.17).
- Complete the Compliance Matrix for Programs/Projects, which "documents the program/projects compliance or intent to comply with the requirements of this NPR or justification for tailoring" and lists, per requirement, the identifier, paragraph reference, requirement statement, rationale, a "Comply?" column, and a tailoring justification column (Appendix H, §H.1.1, p.108).
- Run life-cycle reviews (LCRs) that feed Key Decision Points (KDPs); Chapter 5 sets each review's entrance and success criteria, which NPR 7120.5F's own life-cycle tables point back to (cross-referenced in NPR 7120.5F as "LCR entrance and success criteria in NPR 7123.1").
- Chapter 4 (NASA Systems Engineering Activities on Contracted Projects) sets out what NASA's own technical team does at each stage of a contract — "Prior to Contract Award," "During Contract Performance," "Contract Completion" — which is the chapter most relevant to work performed for NASA by a contractor or FFRDC rather than in-house (our reading, from the chapter's own structure).
- Customizing (choosing *how* a requirement is implemented, as opposed to tailoring, which seeks relief *from* a requirement) "does not require waivers or deviations but should be documented in the program/project SEMP" (§2.2.2.2, p.18).
- Tailoring decisions must be documented "along with supporting rationale that includes the risk evaluation and documented approvals through the Center's ETA process" (§2.2.1.3, p.17).

## Where it stops
- Appendix H is a compliance-tracking template, not an independent inventory of every requirement in the NPR — its own instructions (§H.1.1, p.108) describe it as a matrix a program fills in for its own SEMP, not a list NASA asserts is complete (our reading).
- Verification is explicitly method-agnostic: "Verification (of a product): Proof of compliance with requirements/specifications. Verification may be determined by test, analysis, demonstration, inspection, or a combination thereof." (Appendix A, p.54). Nothing in that definition requires inspecting a product's internals — any of the four methods, including a black-box test or analysis, satisfies it (our reading).
- Validation (of requirements) is defined separately, as a property of the *requirements themselves* before the product exists: "The continuous process of ensuring that requirements are well-formed ... complete ... consistent ... and individually verifiable and traceable to a higher level requirement" (Appendix A, p.54).
- Direct mention of AI by name is thin: "autonom*" appears only a few times, "credibility" twice, and "uncertaint*" once in a text search of the public PDF. "Artificial intelligence" and "machine learning" do not appear.

## AI and machine-learning touchpoints
- §2.2.3.2 lists "Human involvement" as a tailoring consideration, explicitly naming "interaction with, and control/oversight of (semi-) autonomous systems" as a factor a program must weigh when tailoring SE requirements (§2.2.3.2.d, p.18). This is the one place in the document's own words that names autonomous or semi-autonomous systems directly.
- Because the Product Verification (`SE-13`) and Product Validation (`SE-14`) processes are defined around any "end product" without naming a technology, a learned or autonomous component is reached the same way any other component is — through the general process obligation, not through an AI-specific clause (our reading).
- NPR 7123.1D mentions NASA-STD-7009B (Standard for Models and Simulations) only in reference appendices, not as an invoked requirement — so a model-credibility assessment under 7009B is not triggered by this NPR on its own (our reading; see the NASA-STD-7009B page for what does and does not invoke that Standard).

## How it connects to other documents
- P.4 (p.6 area) lists its own applicable documents, including NPR 7120.5 (programmatic management, cross-referenced for life-cycle/KDP structure) and NPR 7150.2 (software engineering requirements) and NASA-HDBK-2203 (the Software Engineering Handbook).
- Its life-cycle review entrance/success criteria are the technical counterpart to NPR 7120.5F's programmatic KDP gates — the two NPRs' life-cycle tables cross-reference each other by name.
- Tailoring and relief route through the Engineering Technical Authority (ETA) process, which is established by NPR 7120.5F §3.3 (see that page's "Technical Authority, quoted" section).
- Appendix C/D are "Reserved" in this revision (p.1 TOC); the SE requirement-to-phase mapping lives in other appendices (E–I) rather than C/D.

## Traps
- **Appendix H is incomplete by design, not by omission.** Some `SE-nn` requirements are tagged in the body text without appearing in Appendix H (for example `SE-01`, `SE-05`, `SE-31`, `SE-33`, `SE-60`–`SE-64`) — consistent with Appendix H being a program/project compliance tool rather than a complete requirement register. Never treat Appendix H as the full list of `SE-nn` requirements.
- Each `SE-nn` tag typically appears twice (once at the clause, once in an appendix mapping table) — a bare text-search count of an `SE-nn` string is not a count of distinct requirements.
- "Technical Authority" is used throughout this NPR (about 20 times) but is not separately defined in its own Appendix A — the definition and the ETA/SMA TA/HMTA role structure live in NPR 7120.5F, not here.
- The document's own convention note (P.2.f, p.5 area) says "are/is" denotes descriptive material, not an obligation — relevant when deciding whether a sentence in this NPR is binding.
