**Scope, plainly.** A 9-page plan describing how NASA implements OMB M-24-10 ("Advancing Governance, Innovation, and Risk Management for Agency Use of Artificial Intelligence"). Verbatim, p.3: *"NASA will publish or update policy directives and requirements to include the roles and responsibilities of the Chief AI Officer. The policy updates will ensure consistency with OMB M-24-10. NASA intends to meet Federal deadlines from EO 14110, OMB M-24-10, and AI Inventory instructions."* It covers AI governance bodies, AI use-case inventories, barriers to responsible AI use, and (per its table of contents, p.2) "Managing Risks from the Use of Artificial Intelligence" and "Determining Which Artificial Intelligence Is Presumed to Be Safety-Impacting or Rights-Impacting."

**Determining safety- or rights-impacting AI, and the waiver path (verbatim, p.7).** *"NASA plans to use the AI definitions from NASA Policy Directive (NPD) documents, NASA Procedure Requirement (NPR) documents, NASA standards, EO14110, OMB M-24-10, and the draft AI Inventory Instructions to 1) assess if a use case meets the definition for AI, and 2) meets the definitions for either safety- or rights-impacts. The CAIO team and AISWG will promulgate these definitions... NASA will utilize existing internal policies and empower organizational leaders and their AI-using Subject Matter Experts (SMEs) to serve as the first tier of assessment."* For a waiver: *"a different sub-team will provide an independent review, the CAIO will provide a review, and the CAIO will team with other approval authorities (e.g., Safety & Mission Assurance, Chief Engineer) to take the appropriate action on the waiver"* (p.7). If AI use must be stopped, *"it can be escalated through the AI governance process to the Deputy Administrator"* (p.8) — the highest escalation point named in the document.

**Named offices/roles and responsibilities (our extraction, verbatim quotes).**
- **Artificial Intelligence Strategy Board (AISB)**, p.3: *"is responsible for defining the vision and strategy for AI, promoting AI adoption and innovation, establishing AI policy and procedural guardrails, identifying AI risk mitigation practices across the Agency, as well as monitoring NASA compliance with Federal guidelines."* Listed functions (a)-(g) include reviewing/approving the NASA AI Strategy, monitoring progress, approving federal AI-reporting submissions, reviewing safety/rights-impacting AI use-case assessments and waivers, and recommending to the Mission Support Council (MSC) on Agency-wide impacts (p.3).
- **Artificial Intelligence Strategic Working Group (AISWG)**, p.4: advises/serves the AISB; responsibilities (a)-(k) include forming AISB discussion topics, "Providing recommendations to the CAIO," developing the AI use-case inventory process, and "Drafting, establishing, and maintaining AI policies."
- **Chief AI Officer (CAIO)**, p.5: in the absence of a named chair, "the CAIO is overseeing an update of the generative AI guidance in Fall 2024," and "the CAIO is postured to issue interim policy memorandums to address concerns and mitigate risk."
- **NASA Chief Information Officer (CIO)**, p.5: "issued initial guidance for use of generative AI in May 2023."
- **Office of Safety and Mission Assurance (OSMA)** and the **Chief Engineer**, p.7-8: named as "other approval authorities" alongside the CAIO in the AI waiver process, and as participants (with "AI leaders in various NASA organizations" and "Chief AI Officer team members") in workshops on safety- and rights-impacting use cases.
- **Deputy Administrator**, p.8: the named escalation point if "directing AI practitioners to cease use of non-compliant AI" becomes necessary.
- Cross-federal: NASA "participates in cross-Federal forums for AI, such as the Chief AI Officers' Council, and three sub-teams" (p.4, p.7).

**Risk management, in the plan's own words (p.8).** *"NASA has extremely robust risk management measures that have evolved and have been refined for decades. These measures are well documented in policies and overseen by risk management boards and NASA Technical Authorities."* It commits to a "plan of action and milestones approach" for non-compliant safety/rights-impacting AI before considering retirement or a waiver, with "directing AI practitioners to cease use of non-compliant AI" as "a last resort" (p.8, quoted). It also names the Agency's "Autonomous Systems Classification Framework" as an existing standard it will keep leveraging "to help guide AI and inform AI quality control measures as well as verification and validation testing" (p.7, quoted).

**AI and machine-learning touchpoints.** Central to the whole document (our reading): "artificial intelligence"/"AI" and "machine learning" occur throughout; "software" appears in the quality-control-of-AI-outputs commitment (p.5, item 3: augmenting "standard engineering, system engineering processes, and software engineering with additional AI considerations").
