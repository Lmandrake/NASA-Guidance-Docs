## What it is for, plainly
NASA's data-governance rulebook: it names who at NASA owns data decisions — the Chief Data Officer (CDO) at the top, then Senior Data Officials, Data Stewards, and Data Custodians underneath — and sets up the boards that coordinate data policy across the agency.

## What it obliges
- §1.1: "The NASA Chief Data Officer (CDO) shall:" serve as the agency's principal data-management official, advise the Administrator on data matters, chair the NASA Data Governance Board (DGB), represent NASA in federal inter-agency CDO forums, and ensure data-rights considerations are embedded in acquisition and operations (p.6).
- §1.2 (unlettered body paragraph): Administrator Staff Office, Mission Directorate, and Mission Support Directorate officials-in-charge, and Center Directors (or their deputies), "shall... Appoint a Senior Data Official to each Administrator Staff Office, MD, MSD, and Center" and "Assure that mission enabling data sets within their organization have an active and accountable Data Steward(s), and Data Custodian(s) assigned" (p.6).
- §1.3: "Senior Data Officials shall:" be accountable for their domain's data needs, define internal controls including Data Management Plans, maintain a data-set inventory, and be accountable for data quality and integrity controls within their domain (pp.6-7).
- §1.5.15.1: when a Data Steward is "acting as a Data Consumer," they must "be responsible for using data in compliance with data governance policies, data privacy laws, ethical guidelines, and any data sharing agreements made with the Data Provider" (p.10; recovered extraction — see Traps).
- Chapter 2: the agency-level Data Governance Board (DGB) "makes decisions regarding data priorities, ownership, program, and projects that enable Data Governance at NASA," and "is part of the IT Strategy Board (ITSB)," with the NASA CDO as "the decision authority of the Data Governance Board" (p.11).
- Chapter 2 also establishes Data Management Boards (DMBs) at the organizational level, which set domain-specific governance "consistent with agency policy," and allows an Enterprise Data Working Group to substitute where no DMB exists; organizations may add further governance layers below the DMB, reporting up to it (pp.11-14).
- Data Stewards "manage day-to-day data lifecycle and quality for a domain" and Data Custodians are "the technical experts administering the systems that store/transfer the data, including API monitoring and access administration" (Chapter 1, pp.7-10).

## Where it stops
- Reaches JPL and other contractors "only to the extent specified or referenced in applicable contracts" (§P.2, p.3) — the directive text alone does not bind an FFRDC.
- Appendix A (Definitions) and Appendix B (Acronyms) did not linearize into readable text in our PDF text extraction, and §1.5.15.1 also does not appear in that same extraction. Both are quoted below from an earlier, separate text extraction of the same document (page markers in that extraction) and should be re-verified against the PDF directly before being treated as authoritative page-for-page.
- The directive sets roles and a federated governance structure; it does not itself set technical data-quality thresholds or data-classification criteria — those are left to the policies the Senior Data Officials and Data Stewards are directed to create (our reading).

## AI and machine-learning touchpoints
- p.15 (recovered extraction; see Where it stops): "Data Access. The ability of a human or Non-Person Entity (NPE) to perform one or more operations on data, typically via service endpoints and Application Programming Interfaces (APIs)." The definition's own wording already reaches a non-human actor by name — "Non-Person Entity" — without any AI-specific carve-out (our reading).
- The CDO's organizational placement — chairing the Data Governance Board, which sits inside the IT Strategy Board under the OCIO (Chapter 2, p.11) — means any data-governance requirement for AI training or operational data would route through the same CDO/DGB chain this directive already sets up, rather than needing a new one (our reading).
- No clause in the extracted text names "artificial intelligence," "machine learning," or a model specifically; data governance here is actor-neutral by the Data Access definition's own wording, not AI-specific (our reading).

## How it connects to other documents
- Senior Data Officials are appointed "as defined in NPD 1000.3" (§1.2, p.6).
- Measurement and verification route through NPD 1001.0 (NASA Data Strategy) and the NASA IT Strategic Plan, with results reported via NASA's annual strategic review, OPEN Data Act reporting, and OMB-directed reporting (§P.5).
- A companion document, NPD 2800.1F (Managing Information Technology), names the NASA Chief Data Officer as working "closely with the NASA Chief Artificial Intelligence (CAIO) Officer" and serving "on the agency artificial intelligence governance" — see the offices cross-reference in this skill's broader notes; not quoted here as this page's own source.

## Traps
- The cover reads "NPD 2800.1," but the NODIS register calls it "NID 2800.149." Cite as "NID/NPD 2800.149" — do not drop the NID half.
- The cover's own "Revised Expiration Date" and the OPD register's extension note give two different December 2026 dates under the same NRW 1400.187 memo (Dec 3 vs. Dec 31) — report both rather than picking one, since neither has been checked against the underlying NRW.
- The Data Access / Non-Person Entity definition and §1.5.15.1 did not extract in our primary PDF pass; both were recovered from a second text extraction of the same public PDF. Treat both page locators as provisional until re-verified against the PDF itself.
