**Scope, plainly.** Twenty pages implementing one of NASA's three Technical Authorities end to end — positional responsibilities (Ch.2), delegation paths (Ch.3), standards/relief adjudication (Ch.4), and formal dissent (Ch.5) — for Health and Medical Technical Authority (HMTA). Its own purpose: "to protect the health and lives of all personnel of an aircraft or a spacecraft from hazards associated with performing flight missions and associated testing and training" (§P.1, p.3). It applies to NASA Headquarters and Centers, reaching JPL "only to the extent specified or referenced in the applicable contract, grant, or agreement" (§P.2.a, p.3). It is the only Technical Authority implementation NPR in this reference set — the Engineering and SMA Technical Authority implementation documents are not reviewed here — so it is this set's model for how a Technical Authority is constituted and exercised, not evidence that HMTA itself reaches AI/ML content (our reading).

**Technical Authority definition.** Appendix A: "Technical Authority (TA). A part of NASA's checks and balances system that provide independent oversight of programs and projects in support of safety and mission success through the selection of individuals at delegated levels of authority. TA delegations are formal and traceable to the Administrator. Individuals with TA are funded independently of a program or project" (Appendix A, "Technical Authority (TA)," p.18).

**CHMO role.** "The CHMO, as the HMTA: a. Establishes and maintains NASA health, medical, and human performance policies and standards for NASA's space flight and atmospheric flight programs and projects. b. Concurs on the establishment of new program or project requirements. c. Certifies program and project compliance with NASA health, medical, and human performance requirements through established Agency processes at life-cycle milestones. d. Provides final decisional authority for any HMTA position or requests for relief..." (§2.1.1, p.7).

**Connections and traps.**
- §5.1 "Diverse Views" and §5.2 "Process for Handling Formal Dissents" (sub-clauses to §5.2.5) give the fullest dissent-process treatment in this reference set — compare NPR 7120.10B §3.1.1's Note, which merely defers to NPR 7120.5 rather than spelling out its own process.
- §4.2 "Adjudication of Requests for Relief from HMTA Standards and Requirements" is this NPR's waiver/deviation mechanism.
- Conclusions about "how a NASA Technical Authority works" drawn from this NPR alone are a single-discipline generalization — the Engineering and SMA Technical Authority implementation NPRs are not in this reference set (our reading).
