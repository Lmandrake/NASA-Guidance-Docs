**Scope, plainly.** Nineteen pages establishing responsibilities, requirements, and processes for selecting, tailoring, and using technical standards as program/project requirements, and for developing and endorsing NASA's own technical standards (§P.1, p.3). It applies to NASA Headquarters and Centers, reaching JPL "only to the extent specified or referenced in the applicable contracts, grants, or agreements" (§P.2.a, p.3), and it explicitly governs standards used under NPR 7120.5, NPR 7120.7, and NPR 7120.8 as well (§P.2.b, p.3).

**§1.2.1 — a standard is not self-imposing.** "A technical standard is not 'self-imposing,' i.e., it is not mandatory for use by a performing entity unless: a. Required by U.S. law or NASA directive, or b. Invoked by other NASA requirements documents (e.g., contracts, including but not limited to, the JPL FFRDC contract, program and project documents, grants, and agreements)" (§1.2.1, p.5). Any argument that a NASA-STD or a voluntary consensus AI standard (e.g., ISO/IEC 42001) reaches a project has to pass through this clause and §3.1.1 below (our reading).

**§3.1.1 — selection order, with Technical Authority concurrence.** "Program and project managers, with delegated Technical Authority concurrence, shall select technical standards... according to the following order of priority: a. Technical standards mandated by law. b. Technical standards designated as mandatory by NPDs and NPRs. c. Technical standards necessary to promote mission success and engineering excellence," and within (c), voluntary consensus standards are preferred over NASA-unique standards "unless inconsistent with applicable laws... technically inadequate, or otherwise impractical" (§3.1.1, p.9). A Note attached to §3.1.1 requires "formal concurrence by the responsible Technical Authorities (Engineering, Safety and Mission Assurance, and/or Health and Medical)... when technical decisions result in residual safety and/or mission success risk," pointing to NPR 7120.5 §§3.3-3.4 for the concurrence and dissent process (p.9).

**§3.2 — Tailoring.** NASA Headquarters offices and program/project managers "shall... [t]ailor, when necessary, technical standards and document necessary changes... for formal approval by the delegated Technical Authority" (§3.2.1.a, p.10), and must document traceability and obtain Technical Authority approval when a technical-authority requirement is tailored (§3.2.1.b, p.10).

**Appendix A — Handbook definition.** "A guidance document that provides engineering information; lessons learned; possible options to address technical issues... Although NASA handbooks may contain 'shall' statements, they are not intended to be requirements documents. Handbooks, or portions thereof, containing procedural or process requirements may be cited in contract, program, and other Agency documents" (Appendix A, "Handbook," p.14).

**Appendix A — Technical Authority definition.** "Part of NASA's system of checks and balances that provides independent oversight of programs and projects in support of safety and mission success through the selection of individuals at delegated levels of authority... Technical Authority delegations are formal and traceable to the Administrator. Individuals with Technical Authority are funded independently of a program or project" (Appendix A, "Technical Authority," pp.15-16, Source cited as NPR 7120.5).

**Document structure.** Preface (P.1-P.6) · Ch.1 Technical Standards (1.1 Overview, 1.2 General Provisions) · Ch.2 Responsibilities (Chief Engineer, HQ sponsoring offices, Mission Directorates/program-project managers, Center Directors) · Ch.3 Selection, Tailoring, and Use (3.1 Selection, 3.2 Tailoring, 3.3 Use, 3.4 Agency's Annual Report on Standards) · Ch.4 Development of Technical Standards (4.1-4.4 development/maintenance, 4.5 NASA-Endorsed Technical Standards) · Appendix A Definitions, B Acronyms, C Reference Documents (pp.1-19).

**Other Appendix A definitions worth citing.** "Government-Unique Standard. A standard developed by and for use by the Federal Government... The standard was not developed as a voluntary consensus standard" (p.14). "Voluntary Consensus Standard. A type of standard developed or adopted by voluntary consensus standards bodies, through the use of a voluntary consensus standards development process..." (pp.16-17). "Technical Authority Requirements. Requirements invoked by OCE, OSMA, and OCHMO documents... These requirements are the responsibility of the office or organization that established the requirement unless delegated elsewhere" (p.16, Source: NPR 7120.5).

**§4.5 — NASA-Endorsed Technical Standards.** "NASA Headquarters offices shall identify and approve technical standards as NASA-endorsed technical standards based on the following criteria: a. The technical standard is approved in accordance with a documented, approved process... b. The technical standard addresses common, high-level functions... c. The technical standard leverages sound engineering practices... d. The technical standard is suitable for wide acceptance by NASA, industry, and other government agencies" (§4.5.1, pp.12-14). A Note promotes this route first: "As a first order, consider NASA-endorsed technical standards, accessible after login at https://standards.nasa.gov, Endorsed Standards" (§3.1.1 Note, p.9).

**Connections and traps.**
- §3.1.1's Note defers the Technical Authority concurrence and dissent *process* to NPR 7120.5 §§3.3-3.4, not to this NPR; this document is the selection mechanism, not the dissent mechanism.
- 🔴 Locator trap: §3.1.1 sits at p.9 (and §3.2 at p.10) of the document; do not cite p.7-8.
- §4.5 is the companion clause for NASA-endorsed technical standards (above); the current endorsed-standards list itself is a login-gated page on standards.nasa.gov, not reproduced here.
- §1.2.4: "Technical standards are accessible at https://standards.nasa.gov" (p.6).
- Appendix C lists this NPR's own reference documents, including NPR 7123.1 and ISO 10795 (p.19).
- A standard selected under §3.1.1(c)(1) in preference to a NASA-unique standard is the in-force clause under which an external AI standard (e.g., ISO/IEC 42001, ISO/IEC 23894) could in principle be selected ahead of a NASA-unique one, if NASA ever designated or developed one (our reading; this NPR does not itself name any AI standard).
